CPU Scheduling Algorithms

CPU scheduling is the basis of multiprogrammed operating systems. By switching the CPU among processes, the operating system can make the computer more productive.

Round Robin scheduling assigns each process a fixed time quantum and cycles through the ready queue, preempting a process once its quantum expires. It is designed for time-sharing systems and guarantees reasonable response time, though the choice of quantum size significantly affects performance: too small and overhead from context switching dominates, too large and it degrades into First-Come-First-Served behavior.

Other common algorithms include Shortest-Job-First, which minimizes average waiting time but requires knowing (or estimating) burst length in advance, and Priority Scheduling, where each process is assigned a priority and the CPU is allocated to the highest-priority ready process, with aging techniques used to prevent starvation of low-priority processes.
